## What we ruled out

1. **Random-action conditioning on still images is structurally broken.** The action-conditioned predictor wastes capacity learning to ignore the noise channel. Verified by sweep A vs B controlled experiment (only difference was action mode; action=zero learned, action=random stayed at chance).

2. **CIFAR-10 is below regime for I-JEPA self-supervised pretraining.** Even with the right hyperparameters (action=zero, mask=0.75, SIGReg) and augmentation (random crop + flip + jitter), linear probe accuracy ceilings around 20-25%. Standard I-JEPA / V-JEPA papers train on millions of frames; 50K is an order of magnitude too few.

3. **LeWM's recipe (no EMA, no stop-grad, SIGReg) needs rich-dynamics data.** It worked for SAI's tabular pretrain (`sai_jepa_v3.py`, 40K-step `jepa_v3_real_final.pt`) and for PushT (the original LeWM paper). On synthetic 2-DOF moving-patch, SIGReg's "make features Gaussian" pressure actively destroys the position-grid structure the predictor needs. Lookahead MSE *worsened* over training (0.46 → 0.75 → 0.78).

4. **CEM with default `init_std=1.0` can't navigate when actions are in [-2, 2].** Most sampled trajectories barely move. The world-model + reward-head MSEs (1.05, 1.31) were also too noisy for CEM to score plans accurately. End-to-end CEM training scored 5% success while pure BC on the same encoder hit 75%+.

5. **Coupling BC to "first 2 dims of predicted next-state"** (as I did in the e2e script) doesn't actually train a deployable policy. CEM doesn't query those dims; it samples actions and scores via reward head. The BC pressure was wasted compute.

## What we validated

- DDP + AMP infrastructure on 6×B200 (12K imgs/s on CIFAR pretrain, 6K imgs/s on movpatch, 18K imgs/s with augmentation pipeline).
- bf16 autocast, no NaN, no overflow.
- SIGReg with N²-cap fix (no OOM at high mask ratios).
- λ warmup, cosine LR, gradient clipping, checkpoint resume.
- ViT encoder + linear head + expert imitation = 100% on navigate.
- Five new training entry points (`scripts/jepa_pretrain.py`, `lewm_temporal_pretrain.py`, `bc_navigate.py`, `end_to_end_navigate.py`, `probe_cifar_class.py`).

## Honest open question

The B.L.A. blueprint promises *more* than what BC alone delivers — world-model rollouts that allow planning past the BC horizon, RAM that recalls relevant past experience, bus that aligns sensorimotor with cognitive plans, veto that catches bad plans. To validate any of those, we need a task where **BC alone can't solve it.** Navigate (as currently configured) is solved by BC at 100%, leaving no headroom for the cognitive components to add value.

Candidate harder tasks (small extensions to the existing env):
1. **Multi-target navigate** — visit N targets in a specific order. Memory matters.
2. **Partial observability** — agent sees a window around itself, not the whole canvas.
3. **Delayed-effect actions** — actions take 2 steps to apply. Planning matters.
4. **Distractor patches** — multiple bright patches; only one is correct (subtle visual cue).

Each is ~30 min of env work + a training run. Without one of these, B.L.A. integration would be ceremonial.
